ANALYSE

The shared functions
of living systems

01

Organization

Living systems are cell-based and maintain an ordered internal environment.

02

Metabolism

They transform matter and energy through linked chemical reactions.

03

Homeostasis

They regulate internal conditions despite external change.

04

Response

They detect stimuli and produce coordinated responses.

05

Growth

They increase in size or cell number through controlled processes.

06

Reproduction

Life continues through replication and transfer of information.

07

Evolution

Populations change across generations through heritable variation.

Scientific precision: Not every individual performs every function at every moment. Sterile organisms remain alive, and dormant seeds may show little visible metabolism. Life is identified from the organization and capacities of the system—not a rigid momentary checklist.

05

APPLY

Classify the mystery

CASE A

A dormant seed

No visible growth today, but cellular organization and genetic information remain intact.

CASE B

A flame

Consumes fuel, releases energy, spreads and responds to airflow—but has no cells or inherited information system.

CASE C

A virus particle

Contains genetic information and evolves, but cannot metabolize or reproduce independently of a host cell.

06

EVALUATE · IB EXAM THINKING

Build a defensible answer

COMMAND TERM · DISCUSS

Discuss whether viruses should be classified as living organisms. [6]

Evidence supporting “living”: viruses contain genetic material, replicate in host cells, show heredity and evolve by natural selection.

Evidence opposing “living”: they are acellular, lack independent metabolism, do not maintain homeostasis and depend on host machinery for reproduction.

Judgement: viruses occupy a boundary. Classification depends on whether life is defined by independent cellular organization or by information, replication and evolution.
